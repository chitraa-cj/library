## English Source

Eşah, this-the Self about whom you ask me; (when) proktah, spoken of; avareņa nareņa, by an inferior man, i.e. by a man of worldly understanding; na hi suvijňeyah, is not certainly liable to be adequately understood; for It is bahudhā, variously-such as "It exists", "It does not exist", "It is the doer", "It is not the doer"; cintyamānah, deliberated on-by disputants. How, again, is It well understood? This is being said: The Self ananyaprokte, when spoken of by a non-different man, by the teacher who does not see duality, who has become identified with the Brahman that is to be revealed (by him¹); atra, here, with regard to the Self; na asti, there does not remain; gatih, cogitation, of various kinds as to whether It exists or not; for from the Self is ruled out all thoughts involving doubt.
